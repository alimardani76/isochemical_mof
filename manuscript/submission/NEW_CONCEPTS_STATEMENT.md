# New Concepts statement

MOF pore design is often compressed into a single “more porosity” or “larger pore” direction, even though surface accessibility, connected void volume, and characteristic diameters encode different structural questions. This work introduces a matched-family way to separate those ideas without treating them as independent causal knobs. After major recorded chemistry and network architecture are fixed before outcome inspection, accessible-space and diameter contrasts occupy different adsorption-response regimes; the value of those regimes is then reorganized by loading condition, framework-density context, and process objective. The new concept is therefore not a better scalar descriptor, but a different design object: a pore-geometry regime defined jointly by geometry mode, geometry magnitude, structural coupling, operating state, and objective. This reframes how large porous-material libraries should be interpreted and how pore engineering should be targeted. Rather than asking which pore metric should be maximized globally, the relevant question is which coupled geometry regime is appropriate for a specified adsorbate and operating task.

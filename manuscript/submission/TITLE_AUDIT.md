# Materials Horizons title audit

## Current title — RECOMMENDED
**Matched MOF Families Reveal Regime-Dependent Pore-Geometry Effects on Adsorption**

### Why it works
- starts with the evidential design rather than a numerical result;
- contains searchable terms: MOF, pore geometry, adsorption;
- communicates regime dependence without claiming causality;
- does not overstate “same chemistry” or atom-by-atom identity;
- remains broad enough to include methane, CO2, pressure, and process analyses;
- is appropriate for a conceptual materials-science pitch.

### Decision
**Keep the current title for submission unless the corresponding author strongly prefers a shorter conceptual title.**

## Alternative A — stronger concept, higher rhetorical risk
**Pore Geometry Is a Regime, Not a Scalar, in Matched MOF Adsorption Families**

Memorable, but more slogan-like and more absolute than the evidence. Better used in New Concepts/cover-letter language than as the formal title.

## Alternative B — shorter and still cautious
**Matched MOF Families Expose Distinct Pore-Geometry Regimes in Adsorption**

Shorter, but loses some of the explicit operating-condition/regime-dependent message.

## Alternative C — highlights the physical split
**Matched MOF Families Separate Accessible-Space and Diameter Responses in Adsorption**

Chemically concrete, but undersells density, state-point, and process-objective findings.

## Final recommendation
**Current title > Alternative B > Alternative A > Alternative C.**

Do not use “isochemical” in the title because the control is a recorded chemistry representation rather than strict atom-by-atom chemical identity.

# Cover letter — Materials Horizons

**Manuscript:** *Matched MOF Families Reveal Regime-Dependent Pore-Geometry Effects on Adsorption*  
**Article type:** Communication

Dear Editors of *Materials Horizons*,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Matched MOF Families Reveal Regime-Dependent Pore-Geometry Effects on Adsorption,”** for publication as a Communication in *Materials Horizons*.

The manuscript addresses a basic materials-design question that is difficult to resolve from conventional high-throughput screening: when major recorded chemistry and broad network architecture are fixed before adsorption outcomes are inspected, what pore-geometry response remains? Rather than ranking frameworks globally, we construct an outcome-blind matched-family comparison in ARC–MOF and freeze one deterministic geometry pair per eligible family before opening the primary adsorption endpoint.

The conceptual advance is that **pore geometry does not behave as one universal scalar design direction**. Accessible-surface and accessible-volume changes occupy response regimes that are distinct from characteristic-diameter changes; those differences persist under cleaner descriptor attribution and remain coupled to framework density. The same frozen structural direction is also re-ordered by loading condition and by the materials objective: capacity gains can coexist with losses in selectivity. We therefore propose that pore engineering should target an adsorbate-, state-, density-, and objective-specific **geometry regime**, rather than maximize one generic pore metric.

We believe this constitutes the type of new way of thinking emphasized by *Materials Horizons*. The result is relevant beyond methane screening because it changes how correlated geometric descriptors in porous-material libraries should be interpreted and how structure–property evidence should be translated into design rules. The chemistry-facing structural examples further show why accessible surface, connected void volume, and characteristic pore diameter should not be collapsed into one visual notion of “pore size.”

We have kept the evidential boundary explicit. The matching controls a recorded chemistry representation and broad architecture rather than atom-by-atom identity; the largest-contrast descriptor is an attribution label rather than a causal perturbation; and the frozen cohort is dominated by hypothetical structures. The manuscript therefore makes a bounded materials-design claim and identifies targeted experimental matched-family validation as the next decisive test.

The analysis, processed verification outputs, figure-generation code, provenance records, and audit trail are organized in the public study repository, while the underlying ARC–MOF source release is cited through its Zenodo record.

Thank you for considering this work for *Materials Horizons*.

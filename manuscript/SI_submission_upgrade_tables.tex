\section{Post-freeze geometry-attribution stability}

The frozen pair driver is the descriptor with the largest symmetric relative
geometry difference in a selected pair. Because the eight pore descriptors can
change simultaneously, an additional post-freeze analysis quantified how clearly
the largest difference exceeded the second largest. No pair was reselected and no
outcome informed the dominance criterion.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Primary CH$_4$ response after progressively requiring the largest pairwise
geometry difference to dominate the second-largest difference.}
\label{tab:S26}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\toprule
Top-1/top-2 criterion & Pairs & Fraction of frozen cohort & Median $\Delta q$ & Positive fraction \\
 & & & (\si{\milli\mole\per\gram}) & \\
\midrule
$\geq1.10$ & 4,603 & 0.4157 & 0.4135 & 0.6626 \\
$\geq1.25$ & 2,866 & 0.2589 & 0.1873 & 0.6015 \\
$\geq1.50$ & 1,715 & 0.1549 & 0.0383 & 0.5312 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The attenuation with increasingly dominant top-1 contrasts indicates that the
largest-difference descriptor is an attribution label rather than evidence of an
isolated one-descriptor perturbation. Strong responses are commonly embedded in
multidimensional geometry changes.

The volumetric result is the informative basis-robustness check because density
enters the uptake representation. The positive median remains well separated from
zero, although the lower positive-pair fraction shows that density moderates
pair-level consistency. The wt\% result is expected to preserve ordering closely
for a fixed adsorbate because it is a mass-basis conversion of molar uptake.
